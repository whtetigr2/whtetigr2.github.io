How occlusion becomes a constraint instead of a computation

Depth cannot be a categorical variable on this kind of fabric. Picking one of D depths needs a one-hot constraint, which is a clique — not two-colourable, and its bias requirement grows with D until it exceeds anything the hardware can express.

So depth is a monotone chain instead. Each cell carries one bit: v[c][k] = 1 means the ray for column c reached depth k still travelling. Three terms, all of them local:

Monotonicity. A cell that has stopped forces everything behind it to have stopped. This is a coupling between vertical neighbours, and it is the whole trick: occlusion stops being something you compute and becomes something the energy forbids. Nothing behind a wall can be seen, because the chain cannot switch back on.

Occupancy. Empty space says keep going. A wall is clamped off — not nudged, clamped. A wall is a constraint, not a preference.

Coherence. Neighbouring columns prefer similar depth, because walls are continuous. This is the term that makes it a lattice rather than a row of independent one-dimensional puzzles, and it is why the result is a draw from a joint distribution rather than a per-column minimum.

Every coupling joins orthogonal neighbours on a grid, so colouring by (column + depth) mod 2 two-colours it exactly: bipartite, degree 4. That is a subgraph of the chessboard these machines are built around, which is why it costs nothing extra to run there.

What this does and does not show

It shows that first-hit visibility can be inferred rather than computed, and that the exact raycast is what the sampler converges to as β rises. The chains start uniformly random every time. The raycast scores the result and is never used to build, seed, or bias the model — if it were, this would demonstrate nothing.

The control is the cold end. At β = 0.05 agreement is zero. If it were high there, the answer would be arriving from somewhere other than the energy, and the whole page would be theatre.

It does not texture, shade, or render a scene. It is 2D and it decides one thing per column. The geometry is input, the way a prompt is input to a sampler; the claim is that visibility is inferred, not that geometry is conjured.

One honest detail: behind the first wall the chain is unconstrained, and you will see cells still reading “travelling” down there. That is not a bug being tolerated — behind a wall the ray never passed, has the ray reached here is a question with no answer, and the energy leaves it open on purpose. The decode reads the first stop, so none of it can reach the result. The number that would matter, and is scored above, is whether a ray ever stops in free space.

What this is, and what it is not

Everything here runs in simulation — in your browser, or on a CPU. Nothing on this site has been run on thermodynamic hardware. No TSU silicon exists for me to test on, so no timing, energy, or throughput figure here describes a physical device, and none is offered as one.

Every number is my own measurement unless it is attributed to someone else, in which case it is theirs and is cited as theirs. I do not treat any of it as established fact until somebody outside this project reproduces it. Where a claim of mine has failed that test, it is corrected in public rather than removed — the repositories keep the retractions alongside the results.

If you find something here that is wrong, that is a favour and I would like to hear it.
