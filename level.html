A level, settled

Walk around. Every wall you see was decided by a lattice relaxing, not by a ray being traced — and what each wall is made of comes out of a second lattice, twelve numbers per material. No image, sprite or texture is stored anywhere on this page. It is shaped like the shooters it is imitating. It contains none of their data.

What is input, and what is inferred

Walking a ray through the level grid to ask is there material at this point is a lookup. That is occupancy, and it is the input — the same way a prompt is input to a sampler. It gets recomputed as you move, because moving changes which points the rays pass through.

Deciding which of those materials you can actually see is a different question, and that is the one being sampled. Each screen column is a chain of bits meaning this ray got this far still travelling, and the surface you see is wherever the chain turned off.

A correction, because this page got it wrong first. It used to say occlusion was “a constraint the energy will not let you break.” It was not. Walls were clamped off, and with a clamp every cell is independent — switching the couplings off entirely changed the answer by exactly zero. The lattice was running beside a linear scan for the nearest wall, contributing nothing. Every control passed, because none of them asked whether the couplings mattered; the check that catches it is an ablation.

A wall is now a strong bias rather than a clamp, so the couplings can outvote occupancy the input got wrong. Measured on this level, agreement with the true nearest surface over 200 columns, seven runs of 150 sweeps each:

clean — couplings off100%
clean — full lattice100%
8% noise — couplings off3.9%
8% noise — full lattice83.5%

On a clean input the couplings are worth nothing. Not nearly nothing — nothing: 100% either way in all seven runs. When the occupancy input already contains the answer, a scan is the right tool and the lattice is decoration, and any honest version of this page has to say so. What the lattice buys is robustness to an input that lies: at 8% misreported cells it takes 3.9% to 83.5%, because walls being continuous and rays not restarting are facts that no single cell holds and no scan can use. That is the part worth having, and it is the only part being claimed.

Both numbers are reproducible on this page: open a console and run ablate(). It re-settles the lattice from random for every cell of the table and prints it.

The chains are not restarted between frames. They carry over and keep relaxing while you move, so the picture is a sampler tracking a changing constraint rather than a fresh solve every frame. Turn quickly and you can watch the far walls resolve a beat behind you. That lag is the sampler working, not a dropped frame.

Why it cannot be anybody's level

There is no pixel data here. Surfaces are coupling constants — twelve numbers for a material — and the creatures are drawn from a small symmetric energy model, so each one differs while staying the same kind of thing. That is the opposite of an asset, which gives you the identical object every time.

The layout is hand-drawn in the spirit of the genre: rooms, a corridor, a courtyard, a zigzag. It is not anyone's map, because reproducing one would need their data, and the point of the exercise is not to have any.

Method and controls for the offline version are in tsu-compiler, under audit/visibility_as_inference.py and audit/texture_from_energy.py.

What this is, and what it is not

Everything here runs in simulation — in your browser, or on a CPU. Nothing on this site has been run on thermodynamic hardware. No TSU silicon exists for me to test on, so no timing, energy, or throughput figure here describes a physical device, and none is offered as one.

Every number is my own measurement unless it is attributed to someone else, in which case it is theirs and is cited as theirs. I do not treat any of it as established fact until somebody outside this project reproduces it. Where a claim of mine has failed that test, it is corrected in public rather than removed — the repositories keep the retractions alongside the results.

If you find something here that is wrong, that is a favour and I would like to hear it.
